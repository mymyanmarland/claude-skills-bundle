# Case Study Writer Skill

Turn client results into persuasive stories that sell.

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs
- **Client name** (or anonymized as "A mid-size retailer in Texas")
- **The challenge/problem** the client had before working with you
- **Your solution**: What exactly did you do?
- **The results**: Specific numbers — revenue, time saved, growth %, cost reduced
- **Timeframe**: How long did it take?
- **Client quote** (if available)
- **Format**: PDF / Web page / Sales deck slide / Short email version

## Step 2 — Case Study Structure

```
## [CLIENT NAME] [optional: logo]
### [Result-focused headline: "How [Company] [achieved X result] in [timeframe]"]

---

## The Client
[2-3 sentences: Who are they? Industry, size, what they do. Set the stage.]

---

## The Challenge
[The problem before you — be specific. What was broken, slow, painful, costly?
Use their language if you have it. Make the reader feel the frustration.]

[Optional client quote about the problem: "[Quote]" — Name, Title]

Key pain points:
- [Pain 1]
- [Pain 2]
- [Pain 3]

---

## The Solution
[What you did and why — your approach, methodology, what made it specific to them.
Not a sales pitch — a narrative of what happened.]

[Phase 1]: [What you did]
[Phase 2]: [What happened next]
[Phase 3]: [Final implementation]

---

## The Results

[Lead with the best number:]
**[Big result — e.g., "43% increase in revenue in 90 days"]**

| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|--------|--------|-------|--------|
| [Metric 1] | [X] | [Y] | [+Z%] |
| [Metric 2] | [X] | [Y] | [+Z%] |
| [Metric 3] | [X] | [Y] | [+Z%] |

[Client quote about the outcome:]
"[Quote — result-focused, enthusiastic, specific]"
— [Name], [Title], [Company]

---

## What's Next
[Optional: what the client is doing now, continued partnership, next phase]

---

*[CTA: Want results like these? → Book a call / Contact us / See more case studies]*
```

## Step 3 — Case Study Rules
- Lead with results, not process
- Specific numbers beat vague claims ("3x ROI" beats "significantly improved")
- Client voice is gold — use direct quotes wherever possible
- Keep it under 600 words for web; longer PDF versions (1000-1500) for sales use
- Always anonymize if client hasn't approved public use

Always offer: "Want a shorter 150-word version for a sales email or proposal?"
