# Infographic Copy Writer Skill
## Step 1 — Gather Inputs
- **Topic**: What is the infographic about?
- **Type**: Statistical / Process / Timeline / Comparison / How-to / List / Map
- **Target audience**
- **Key message**: What should someone take away at a glance?
- **Data or facts to include**: (or research and suggest)
- **Brand voice**: Formal / Casual / Technical / Educational

## Step 2 — Infographic Copy Structure
```
## Infographic Copy: [Topic]
Type: [X] | Audience: [X]

---

### HEADLINE
[Under 8 words — bold, clear, contains the main insight]
[A stat, a question, or a claim — not a description]

### SUBHEADLINE
[One line that expands on the headline — 10-15 words max]

---

### SECTION 1: [Header — 2-4 words]
[Data point or key fact: number + context in under 15 words]
[Caption/explanation: under 20 words]

### SECTION 2: [Header]
[Data point]
[Caption]

### SECTION 3: [Header]
[Data point]
[Caption]

[Continue for all sections — typically 4-7 for a standard infographic]

---

### CALLOUT STATS (large numbers to highlight)
• [Stat 1]: [Brief label]
• [Stat 2]: [Brief label]
• [Stat 3]: [Brief label]

### SOURCE LINE
[Data source citations — essential for credibility]

### CTA (bottom)
[One action: Visit [URL] / Download [resource] / Share this / Learn more at [X]]

---

### COPY WORD COUNTS (for designer reference)
Headline: [X] words
Total body copy: ~[X] words
```

## Step 3 — Infographic Copy Rules
- **Every word must earn its place** — no filler
- **Numbers > words** wherever possible: "73%" not "nearly three-quarters"
- **Parallel structure in lists**: if one bullet starts with a verb, all do
- **The headline does 60% of the work** — spend the most time here
- **Source everything** — infographics spread; wrong data spreads faster

Always offer: "Want me to write social media captions to promote this infographic?"
