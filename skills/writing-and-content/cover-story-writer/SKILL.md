# Cover Story Writer Skill
## Step 1 — Gather Inputs
- **Subject**: Person / Company / Trend / Event / Place / Idea
- **Publication or context**: Consumer magazine / Trade publication / Brand content / Blog
- **Audience**: General / Industry professional / Consumer / Fan
- **Length**: 800 / 1500 / 2500 / 4000+ words
- **Angle**: What's the specific story — not just "about X" but the unique lens
- **Available quotes, facts, or research**

## Step 2 — Feature Article Structure
```
[SCENE-SETTING LEAD — drop the reader into a specific moment, not background]
[2-4 paragraphs: sensory detail, action, a person doing something real]

[NUT GRAPH — "this story is really about..."]
[1-2 paragraphs: the point of the piece, why it matters now, the thesis]

[SECTION 1 — The context/background (told as narrative, not Wikipedia)]
[Subhead if long-form]

[SECTION 2 — The heart of the story: conflict, tension, or the interesting thing]

[SECTION 3 — Supporting evidence, other voices, complications]

[SECTION 4 — What it means / implications / what happens next]

[CLOSING — return to the opening scene or image, or a final quote/moment that lands]
[Never end with "only time will tell"]
```

## Step 3 — Feature Writing Rules
- **Never start with "In today's world..."** — it's the single most tired opener
- **The anecdote lead**: Drop into a specific scene first, explain why it matters second
- **Quotes advance the story** — don't use quotes for information you could paraphrase
- **The nut graph** (paragraph 3-5): This is where you tell the reader what the piece is about
- **Subheads every 400-600 words** in long-form — readers scan before they commit

Always offer: "Want me to write just the opening 3 paragraphs to hook an editor?"
