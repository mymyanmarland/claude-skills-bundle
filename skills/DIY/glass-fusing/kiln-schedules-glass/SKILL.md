# Glass Kiln Schedules Skill

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

- Craft experience level
- Specific project or technique
- Materials and tools available
- Budget
- Intended use: personal / gift / sale

## Step 2 — Core Output

Glass kiln schedules: Rate of Advance ROA (degrees per hour — slow for thick glass) → Phases of a fuse schedule (preheat / process phase / rapid cooling / anneal soak / final cooling) → Annealing (cooling through the annealing range slowly — prevents internal stress that causes spontaneous breaking) → Full fuse schedule (typically 815°C / tack fuse: 700-760°C) → Processing temperature variation (thicker glass / lower temperature / longer hold) → Digital controller (most modern kilns have programmable controllers — use them).

## Step 3 — Quality & Polish

- Annealing is non-negotiable: glass that is not annealed is fragile and may break spontaneously
- Slow preheat for thick glass: thermal shock cracks thick pieces during heating
- Kiln controller: programme the schedule — do not rely on manual switching

Always offer a relevant next step or tip to try.

---
📌 Safety Disclaimer: Glass fusing involves high temperatures and sharp glass. Always wear safety glasses when cutting glass, use appropriate gloves, follow all kiln manufacturer safety instructions, and ensure adequate ventilation. Never leave a kiln unattended during firing.
