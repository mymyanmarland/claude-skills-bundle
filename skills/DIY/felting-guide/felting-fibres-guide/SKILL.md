# Felting Fibres and Materials Guide Skill

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

- Craft experience level
- Specific project or technique
- Materials and tools available
- Budget
- Intended use: personal / gift / sale

## Step 2 — Core Output

Felting fibres: What felts well (wool / alpaca / mohair — protein fibres with scales) → What does not felt (superwash wool: scales removed / most synthetics / cotton / linen) → Merino wool (felts readily / soft / good for next-to-skin felt items) → Corriedale (more forgiving / slightly coarser / good for beginners) → Coarser wools (Romney / Shetland — faster felting / more durable felt) → Fibre forms (batting / roving / carded fleece / lock form) → Colour (undyed natural / hand-dyed / commercially dyed batts — each has different felting behaviour).

## Step 3 — Quality & Polish

- Superwash wool does not felt: the scales have been removed — a common and costly mistake
- Merino for soft felt: lower micron count produces a softer finer felt
- Corriedale for beginners: more forgiving than fine Merino during the learning process

Always offer a relevant next step or tip to try.
