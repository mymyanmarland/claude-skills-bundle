# Monotype and Monoprint Techniques Skill

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

- Craft experience level
- Specific project or technique
- Materials and tools available
- Budget
- Intended use: personal / gift / sale

## Step 2 — Core Output

Monotype and monoprint: Monotype (paint or ink on a plate / print once — unique / no repeat possible) → Ghost print (the ink remaining on the plate after the first print / second print is lighter — print the ghost too) → Monoprint (repeatable plate with unique additions / lino base plus painted additions) → Plate materials (glass / perspex / sheet metal — smooth surfaces) → Additive method (build up with ink or paint on the plate) → Subtractive method (cover the plate with ink / draw through to the plate surface with a tool) → Layering (allow each layer to dry / overprint for depth).

## Step 3 — Quality & Polish

- Ghost print: always print the ghost — it has its own quality
- Subtractive method: scratching into a rolled plate is the most intuitive starting point
- Monotypes are truly unique: the most painterly of print processes
