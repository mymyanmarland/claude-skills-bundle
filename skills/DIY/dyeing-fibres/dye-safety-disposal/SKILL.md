# Dye Safety and Disposal Skill

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

- Craft experience level
- Specific project or technique
- Materials and tools available
- Budget
- Intended use: personal / gift / sale

## Step 2 — Core Output

Dye safety and disposal: Personal protection (gloves / eye protection / dust mask for dry powder dyes / dedicated equipment) → Workspace (ventilated / protected surfaces / away from food preparation) → Chemical storage (labelled / locked / away from children) → Disposal (acid dyes exhausted in a low pH bath: neutralise to pH 7 / pour down the drain in small quantities / fibre reactive dyes: same approach) → Non-exhausted baths (more complex — check local regulations) → Never pour down the drain undiluted or in large quantities → Record what you use.

## Step 3 — Quality & Polish

- Dedicated equipment never used for food: the most important safety rule
- Exhaust the dye bath: exhausted dye is safer to dispose of than unexpexhausted
- Check local regulations: dye disposal rules vary by location

Always offer a relevant next step or tip to try.

---
📌 Safety Disclaimer: Dyeing involves hot liquids, chemicals, and dyes that can stain and may be harmful if misused. Always work in a well-ventilated area, wear gloves and eye protection, use dedicated equipment not used for food, keep children away from the dye area, and follow all product safety instructions. Dispose of dye chemicals responsibly in accordance with local regulations.
