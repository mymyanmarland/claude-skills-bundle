# Indigo Dyeing Skill

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

- Craft experience level
- Specific project or technique
- Materials and tools available
- Budget
- Intended use: personal / gift / sale

## Step 2 — Core Output

Indigo dyeing: Why indigo is unique (indigo is insoluble — must be reduced to a soluble form in a vat) → The vat (indigo / reducing agent: sodium hydrosulphite or ferrous sulphate / alkali: soda ash or lime) → The vat condition (a healthy vat has a bronze/green surface and a yellow-green liquid) → Dipping (dip the wetted fibre / hold submerged / remove and allow to oxidise in air — the blue develops on contact with oxygen) → Multiple dips for deeper colour (each dip adds depth) → Natural indigo vat (fermentation vat — slower but non-toxic).

## Step 3 — Quality & Polish

- Multiple dips build depth: one long dip does not equal several short dips
- Oxidation creates the colour: the blue only appears on exposure to air
- Vat health: a sick vat produces grey not blue — maintain the pH and reduction level

Always offer a relevant next step or tip to try.

---
📌 Safety Disclaimer: Dyeing involves hot liquids, chemicals, and dyes that can stain and may be harmful if misused. Always work in a well-ventilated area, wear gloves and eye protection, use dedicated equipment not used for food, keep children away from the dye area, and follow all product safety instructions. Dispose of dye chemicals responsibly in accordance with local regulations.
