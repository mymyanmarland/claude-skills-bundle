# Plumbing Safety and Isolation Guide Skill

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

- Skill level: beginner / some experience / confident DIYer
- Specific task or project
- Materials and tools available
- Budget
- Any safety or structural concerns

## Step 2 — Core Output

Plumbing safety: Mains stopcock location (usually under the kitchen sink — know where it is before a leak happens) → Individual isolating valves (flat-head screw on supply pipes — quarter turn to close) → Water meter (outside — property's ultimate isolation point) → Draining down (open the lowest tap after isolation to drain the system) → Hot water (turn off the boiler / allow water to cool before working on hot pipes) → Gravity-fed vs pressurised systems (cold water tank in loft feeds some systems — isolate at the tank or tie the ballcock up) → When to call a plumber (gas pipes / boiler work / major leaks / any uncertainty).

## Step 3 — Quality & Polish

- Know the stopcock before an emergency: locate it now — not during a flood
- Individual isolating valves: allow work without draining the whole system
- Gas and boiler work: always a qualified professional — no exceptions

Always offer a relevant next step or tip to try.

---
📌 Plumbing Safety Disclaimer: Always turn off the water supply before starting any plumbing work. For gas pipes, boilers, or any work you are unsure about, hire a qualified plumber or gas engineer. Poor plumbing can cause flooding, structural damage, or health hazards — seek professional advice when in doubt.
