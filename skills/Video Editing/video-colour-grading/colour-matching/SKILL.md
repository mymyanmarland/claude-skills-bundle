# Colour Matching Footage Skill

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

- Software version and platform (Windows/Mac)
- Experience level: beginner/intermediate/advanced
- Type of project: social media/film/documentary/YouTube/corporate
- Specific task or technique
- Output format or delivery requirement

## Step 2 — Core Output

Colour matching: Why clips differ (different cameras / different lenses / different lighting setups) → Reference frame (choose the best-lit most correctly exposed clip as the reference) → Waveform comparison (open two clips side by side / match the Waveform shapes) → Colour Warper (Resolve: node type that maps the colour gamut of one clip to another) → Match Shot (Premiere and Resolve both have auto-match features — as a starting point) → Skin tone as the reference (the skin tone on the Vectorscope is the most reliable cross-clip reference) → Camera LUT first (convert all cameras to Rec.709 before matching — same starting point).

## Step 3 — Quality & Polish

- Convert to Rec.709 first: matching log footage is much harder than matching Rec.709 footage
- Waveform comparison: the shape of the Waveform tells you how to adjust
- Reference frame selection: the most important decision in colour matching — choose well
