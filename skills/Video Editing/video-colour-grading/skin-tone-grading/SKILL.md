# Skin Tone Grading Skill

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

- Software version and platform (Windows/Mac)
- Experience level: beginner/intermediate/advanced
- Type of project: social media/film/documentary/YouTube/corporate
- Specific task or technique
- Output format or delivery requirement

## Step 2 — Core Output

Skin tone grading: Vectorscope skin tone indicator (a diagonal line on the Vectorscope — skin tones of all ethnicities fall on this line when correctly graded) → Skin tone qualification (use a secondary qualifier to isolate skin tones) → Hue rotation (move skin tones toward orange if too green / toward yellow if too red) → Saturation of skin (increase saturation if skin looks grey / reduce if too red) → Luminance (skin should be the brightest area of a portrait — check the Waveform) → Cross-platform consistency (check grades on multiple monitors and devices).

## Step 3 — Quality & Polish

- Vectorscope skin tone line: the universal reference regardless of skin colour
- Qualify before adjusting skin: affect only the skin — not similar colours in the scene
- Skin as the brightest area: a portrait where the background is brighter than the face looks wrong
