# Keynote Speech Writer Skill
## Step 1 — Gather Inputs
- **Event and audience**: (industry / occasion / approximate size)
- **Speaker's role and credibility**: (why them for this event?)
- **Central theme or message**: (what should people leave thinking/feeling/doing?)
- **Length**: 20 / 30 / 45 / 60 minutes
- **Tone**: Inspiring / Strategic / Challenging / Celebratory / Visionary
- **Stories or examples to include**

## Step 2 — Keynote Speech Structure
```
## Keynote Speech: "[Title]"
Event: [X] | Audience: [X] | Length: [X min]

---

### OPENING (5% of time)
[A commanding presence opener — not "good morning, it's great to be here"]
[Start with something that earns their attention immediately]
[Establish what this speech will give them — the promise]

---

### THE EMOTIONAL FOUNDATION (15%)
[The story or truth that makes the audience lean in]
[This is where trust is built — authentic, personal, human]
[The best keynotes make a room of strangers feel like one community]

---

### THE INTELLECTUAL CORE (50%)
[The ideas, frameworks, data, or insights that deliver the promise]
[Organized in 3 sections — rule of three is optimal for retention]
[Each section: insight + story + implication]

Section 1: [Title]
Section 2: [Title]
Section 3: [Title]

---

### THE CHALLENGE (15%)
[Don't just inform — ask something of the audience]
[The best keynotes send people back to their organizations changed]
[Specific, actionable, appropriate to their power/role]

---

### THE CLOSE (15%)
[The emotional crescendo — bring it all together]
[Callback to the opening]
[The final image or sentence that they carry out of the room]

Closing line: "[Draft]"
```

## Step 3 — Keynote Rules
- The audience isn't there to learn — they're there to be moved to action
- Every story must serve the message — ruthlessly cut stories that don't
- The close is as important as the open — both need writing, rewriting, and memorization
- Pause for effect — silence is the most underused tool in keynote speaking

Always offer: "Want me to write speaker notes for the most important transitions?"
