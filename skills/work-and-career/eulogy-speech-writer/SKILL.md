# Eulogy & Speech Writer Skill

Write speeches that say what the heart means — clearly, memorably, and in the speaker's own voice.

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

Ask warmly and gently, especially for eulogies. Ask in one message:
- **Occasion**: Wedding / Funeral / Graduation / Retirement / Birthday milestone / Toast / Award / Other
- **Speaker's relationship to the honoree**: Best friend, sibling, parent, colleague, child, etc.
- **The person being honored**: Name, personality, what makes them special
- **2–3 specific memories or stories** about the person (the more specific, the better the speech)
- **Tone**: Warm & emotional / Celebratory & joyful / Humorous & light / Mix of humor and heart / Deeply reverent
- **Length**: Short (2–3 min) / Medium (4–6 min) / Long (7–10 min)
- **Anything specific to include or avoid**

## Step 2 — Speech Structures by Occasion

### 🕊️ Eulogy (Funeral / Memorial)

```
## 🕊️ Eulogy Structure: [Name]

**Tone**: Warm, honest, celebratory of a life — grief acknowledged but not dwelling

---

**Opening** (acknowledge the room, not a cold start):
[A line that gently enters the moment — acknowledge that we're all here because of loss,
and because of love. Don't open with a joke at a funeral unless you knew the deceased
very well and it would honor them specifically.]

**Who they were** (not a biography — an impression):
[Not a list of facts, but how they made people feel. What was the experience of knowing them?
One or two defining qualities, illustrated with a story.]

**The story** (the heart of the eulogy):
[One specific, sensory memory — a scene, a conversation, a moment.
Specific beats what general every time. "He always made people laugh" < 
"The last time I saw him, he was telling a terrible pun to a complete stranger at the
grocery store — and the stranger was laughing."]

**What they taught you / their legacy**:
[Not a platitude — what specific thing about this person changed you or the people around them?
What will you carry forward?]

**Acknowledgment of grief**:
[Give the loss its due — a sentence or two that lets the room feel what they're feeling.
Don't rush past the grief to reach comfort.]

**The close** (a send-off, not just an ending):
[A final image, a line they used to say, a promise to remember, a direct address to the person —
something that gives the speech a landing rather than just stopping.]
```

### 💍 Wedding Speech (Best Man / Maid of Honor / Parent / Couple)

```
## 💍 Wedding Speech Structure

**Tone**: Warm, celebratory, funny if appropriate — always ends on love

---

**Opening** (get the room on your side immediately):
[Introduce yourself briefly — who you are and how you know the couple.
Optional: a light self-deprecating opener. Don't open with "I was told to keep this short..."]

**The story of how you know them**:
[When you met, first impression — bonus if the first impression was wrong in a funny way]

**What makes them remarkable** (the one you know best):
[2–3 specific qualities with brief illustrations — not generic praise. "Kind" < 
"She once drove 4 hours to bring me soup when I had the flu. I hadn't even asked."]

**The love story moment** (what you noticed when they met or fell in love):
[When did you see them change? What did you notice? This is usually the emotional peak.]

**Welcome to the family/group**:
[Words for the partner you're "receiving" — what you've observed about them, why they're right]

**Advice or wish** (optional — keep it genuine, not generic):
[One piece of wisdom or wish for their life together — specific to them, not a cliché]

**The toast**:
[End with the toast — raise the glass. Make the last line something people want to repeat.]

**Best man speech extra**:
[Tradition allows 1–2 gentle jokes at groom's expense — make them affectionate, not humiliating.
Clear the speech with the groom first if there's any doubt.]
```

### 🎓 Graduation Speech

```
## 🎓 Graduation Speech Structure

**Tone**: Inspiring, honest, personal — not generic commencement platitudes

---

**Opening** (earn attention immediately):
[Start with a specific moment, story, or question — not "Webster's dictionary defines..."]

**Acknowledge the journey**:
[What was hard about getting here? What did this class overcome?]

**The real lesson** (the one not in the curriculum):
[What did you actually learn — about yourself, about people, about life?
The most memorable graduation speeches name something true and unexpected.]

**The honest challenge ahead**:
[What's actually hard about what comes next? Don't sugarcoat. Graduating classes respect honesty.]

**What you're carrying forward**:
[One specific value, memory, or lesson from this time that you'll hold onto]

**The close** (a charge, not just a goodbye):
[Send them forward with something to hold — a challenge, a permission, a vision]
```

### 🎉 Retirement Speech / Tribute

```
## 🎉 Retirement Speech Structure

**Tone**: Celebratory, grateful, warmly retrospective

---

**Opening**: Thank the room and orient people to the relationship
**The career in story form**: Not a resume — the moments that defined their contribution
**The person behind the professional**: What colleagues know that the job title doesn't capture
**The impact**: What's different because they were here — specific and true
**What they're taking into retirement**: What you hope for their next chapter
**The send-off**: A toast or final words that honor the transition
```

### 🥂 Short Toast (60–90 seconds)

```
## 🥂 Toast Formula

For any celebratory occasion, a great toast has 4 parts:

1. **Relationship anchor** (1 sentence): "For those who don't know me, I'm [name] — [relationship to honoree]."

2. **The one thing** (2–3 sentences): The single most true and specific thing about this person.
   Not "She's amazing" — but what makes her amazing, in a brief story or image.

3. **The wish** (1–2 sentences): What you hope for them — specific to their situation.

4. **The lift** (the toast itself): A sentence ending in the raise:
   "So please raise your glass to [name] — [final line that's worth repeating]."

**Total**: 60–90 seconds spoken. Never longer for a toast.
```

## Step 4 — Speech Writing Principles

```
## ✍️ What Makes a Great Speech

**Specific always beats general**:
❌ "She was so kind to everyone."
✅ "The first time I met her, she spent 20 minutes helping a lost tourist find their hotel.
    She was late to our own lunch. She didn't mention it."

**One great story beats five good ones**:
Resist the urge to list everything. Choose the one story that captures the person.
A single vivid scene is worth ten summaries.

**Write how you talk, not how you write**:
Read it aloud while writing. If you stumble — rewrite.
Contractions are good. Short sentences are good. "I" is allowed.

**Earn the emotion**:
Don't tell people to cry. Tell the specific truth — and let them feel it themselves.
"He loved her so much" is telling. "He kept a photo of her in his wallet for 43 years
and showed it to anyone who asked about his family" is showing.

**The ending matters most**:
The last line is what people remember. Write it first. Work backward.

**Handling nerves**:
- Practice aloud at least 5 times — not just reading silently
- It's okay to cry — pausing and continuing is always more moving than holding it together
- If you feel emotion rising, look up, take a breath, and continue
- The audience wants you to succeed — they're with you
```

## Step 5 — Final Checklist

```
## ✅ Before You Deliver

- [ ] Read aloud — does it sound like you talking, or someone else?
- [ ] Timed at natural pace — within target length?
- [ ] Opening line memorized (most nervous moment = most important to know cold)
- [ ] Closing line memorized (this is what they'll remember)
- [ ] Any jokes: tested on 1–2 people who know the room
- [ ] Printed in large font (16pt+) with double spacing — easy to read while nervous
- [ ] Practice with the actual paper/device you'll use
- [ ] One glass of water nearby (dry mouth is real)
- [ ] Told someone what you're about to say (reduces isolation anxiety)
```

Always offer: "Want me to adjust the tone or length?" or "Want a shorter version to use as a backup if you get emotional?"
