# Performance Review Writer Skill

Write reviews that are specific, evidence-based, and genuinely useful — not generic filler.

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

Ask in one message if not provided:
- **Who is writing** (employee self-review / manager writing about direct report)
- **Review period** (Q1, H1, annual, etc.)
- **Role and level** (job title and seniority)
- **Key accomplishments** during the period (even rough notes are fine)
- **Challenges faced** and how they were handled
- **Goals from last review** — were they met?
- **Areas for growth** (what they want to develop)
- **Rating scale** (if applicable — 1–5, Exceeds/Meets/Below, etc.)
- **Word limit or format** (free-form, structured sections, specific prompts)

---

## Step 2 — Self-Review Framework (Employee)

### The STAR-I Method for Each Achievement
- **S**ituation: What was the context?
- **T**ask: What were you responsible for?
- **A**ction: What did YOU specifically do?
- **R**esult: What was the measurable outcome?
- **I**mpact: What did this mean for the team/company/customers?

---

### Self-Review Sections

#### 1. Key Accomplishments

```
## 🏆 Key Accomplishments — [Period]

### [Accomplishment 1 Title]
**Context**: [Brief situation — what challenge or opportunity existed]
**My contribution**: [What I specifically did — use "I", not "we"]
**Result**: [Quantified outcome — % improvement, revenue, time saved, etc.]
**Impact**: [What this meant beyond the number — team efficiency, customer satisfaction, company goal]

### [Accomplishment 2 Title]
[Same structure]

### [Accomplishment 3 Title]
[Same structure]
```

**Quantification guide when you don't have exact numbers**:
- "Led a team of X people"
- "Delivered project X weeks ahead of schedule"
- "Reduced process from X steps to Y steps"
- "Handled X% of team's client escalations"
- "Trained X new team members"
- "Contributed to team that achieved [company milestone]"

## Step 3 — Manager Review Framework

### Writing Fair, Useful Manager Reviews

```
## 📋 Performance Review: [Employee Name]
**Role**: [Title] | **Period**: [Date range] | **Reviewer**: [Manager name]
**Overall Rating**: [Rating] — [Brief justification in one sentence]

---

### Performance Summary
[2–3 paragraph overview: What they were responsible for this period, how they showed up overall, what stood out most — positive and developmental]

---

### Key Strengths

**[Strength 1]**:
[Specific behavioral evidence from the period — not generic praise]
*Example*: "In Q3, when [situation occurred], [Name] took initiative to [action], which resulted in [outcome]."

**[Strength 2]**:
[Evidence-based observation]

---

### Areas for Development

**[Area 1]**:
[Observed behavior or gap — described neutrally and factually]
*What good looks like*: [Specific description of expected behavior]
*Suggested action*: [Concrete, supportive next step]

**[Area 2]**:
[Same structure]

---

### Goals Achievement

| Goal | Rating | Evidence |
|------|--------|----------|
| [Goal from last review] | Exceeded / Met / Partially Met / Not Met | [Specific evidence] |

---

### Goals for Next Period

| Goal | Success Criteria | Timeline |
|------|-----------------|----------|
| [Goal] | [Measurable outcome] | [Date] |

---

### Overall Comments
[Forward-looking, motivating close — what you believe they're capable of, what you're committed to supporting]
```

## Step 4 — Language Guide

### Words to USE (specific, active, impactful)
- Delivered, achieved, exceeded, built, led, drove, reduced, improved, designed, launched, resolved, influenced, mentored, streamlined

### Words to AVOID (vague, meaningless)
- "Hardworking", "team player", "passionate", "always", "never", "good attitude", "tries hard"

### For manager reviews — avoid these traps:
- **Recency bias**: Don't only cite the last month — review the full period
- **Halo effect**: One great project doesn't mean all areas are great
- **Similarity bias**: Don't rate people higher just because they work like you
- **Vague positives**: "Great communicator" → instead: "Consistently distills complex data into exec-ready summaries"
- **Vague negatives**: "Needs to improve communication" → instead: "In Q2, three stakeholders flagged receiving project updates too late; this is an area to prioritize in H2"

## Step 5 — Self-Advocacy Tips

```
## 💡 Self-Review Pro Tips

1. **Track accomplishments year-round** — keep a "brag doc" with wins as they happen
2. **Use "I" not "we"** — your review is about your contribution, not the team's
3. **Quantify even approximations** — "roughly 30% faster" beats "significantly faster"
4. **Address gaps proactively** — name your development areas before your manager does; it shows self-awareness
5. **Connect to company goals** — tie your work to the bigger picture (OKRs, mission, strategy)
6. **Don't be modest** — no one else is advocating for you in this document
7. **Align with your manager first** — know what they value before you write
```

Always offer: "Want me to strengthen any specific section?" or "Want me to write this in a specific format your company uses?"
