# Cover Letter Writer Skill

Write a cover letter that gets read — specific, human, and connected to what the employer actually cares about.

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

Ask in one message if not provided:
- **Job title and company name**
- **Job description** (or key requirements)
- **Candidate's resume or top 3 relevant experiences**
- **Why this company specifically** (what do they know/like about it?)
- **Tone preference** (formal and traditional / warm and conversational / bold and confident)
- **Word limit** (default: 300–400 words, 3–4 paragraphs)

---

## Step 2 — Pre-Writing Analysis

Before writing, extract from the job description:

```
## 🔍 Job Fit Analysis

**Top 3 things they need**:
1. [Requirement 1]
2. [Requirement 2]
3. [Requirement 3]

**Candidate's matching evidence**:
1. → [Specific experience/achievement that proves this]
2. → [Specific experience/achievement]
3. → [Specific experience/achievement]

**Company angle** (what makes this company worth calling out):
- [Mission / recent news / product / culture signal from JD]
```

---

## Step 3 — Cover Letter Structure

### The 4-Paragraph Formula

**Paragraph 1 — The Hook (Why this role, why now)**
- Open with something specific — not "I am writing to apply for..."
- Reference the company by name; mention something real about them
- State the role clearly
- One sentence on why you're a strong fit (the preview)

❌ Weak opening: *"I am writing to express my interest in the Marketing Manager position at ABC Corp."*
✅ Strong opening: *"When I read that ABC Corp is expanding into Southeast Asian markets this year, I immediately thought of the 3 years I spent building brand presence across Malaysia and Singapore — and why the Marketing Manager role feels like exactly the right next chapter."*

**Paragraph 2 — Your Strongest Evidence**
- Pick your single most relevant achievement
- Tell the story briefly: context → action → result
- Use numbers wherever possible
- Connect directly to what the job requires

**Paragraph 3 — Why This Company (not just any company)**
- Show genuine research: recent news, their product, their mission, their culture
- Explain what about this specific company appeals to you
- Make it impossible to send this letter to a different company

**Paragraph 4 — The Close**
- Confident call to action (not apologetic)
- Express enthusiasm without desperation
- Mention availability / next steps
- Thank them briefly

## Step 4 — Full Cover Letter Output

```
## 📝 Cover Letter: [Name] → [Role] at [Company]

---

[City, Date]

Hiring Manager
[Company Name]

Dear [Hiring Manager name / Hiring Team],

[PARAGRAPH 1 — Hook: specific opening tied to company + role + your fit preview]

[PARAGRAPH 2 — Strongest evidence: one achievement told as a brief story with result]

[PARAGRAPH 3 — Why this company: specific, researched, genuine reason]

[PARAGRAPH 4 — Confident close: call to action, enthusiasm, thank you]

Sincerely,
[Full Name]
[Phone] | [Email] | [LinkedIn]

---
Word count: ~XXX words
```

---

## Step 5 — Tone Variants

Offer 2–3 opening paragraph variants with different tones when asked:

**Formal**:
*"I am pleased to submit my application for the [Role] position at [Company]. With [X] years of experience in [field], I bring a track record of [result] that aligns directly with the goals outlined in your job description."*

**Warm/Conversational**:
*"I've followed [Company]'s work in [space] for years, so when I saw the [Role] opening, I knew I had to apply. My background in [area] — particularly [specific thing] — feels like it was built for exactly this kind of challenge."*

**Bold/Confident**:
*"[Company] is tackling [problem] in a way that no one else is — and I want to be part of it. As someone who has [specific achievement], I know I can contribute meaningfully from day one."*

---

## Step 6 — Cover Letter Rules

```
## ✅ Cover Letter Quality Check
- [ ] Opens with something specific — not "I am writing to apply"
- [ ] Company name used at least twice (shows it's not generic)
- [ ] At least one quantified achievement mentioned
- [ ] No paragraph starts with "I" (vary sentence structure)
- [ ] No clichés: "team player", "hardworking", "passion for excellence"
- [ ] Under 400 words (hiring managers don't read long letters)
- [ ] Tone matches company culture (startup vs. law firm vs. NGO)
- [ ] Closing is confident, not apologetic ("I look forward to" not "I hope you might consider")
- [ ] Tailored — cannot be sent to a different company without editing
```

Always offer: "Want me to adjust the tone?" or "Want a version for a different role at the same company?"
