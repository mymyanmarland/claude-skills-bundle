# Salary Negotiation Coach Skill

Prepare for salary negotiations with strategy, scripts, and confidence.

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

Ask in one message if not provided:
- **Scenario**: New job offer / asking for raise / promotion negotiation / counter-offer
- **Current or offered salary** (and currency)
- **Target salary** (what they actually want)
- **Years of experience** in this field
- **Location** (city/country — affects market rates significantly)
- **Industry and role**
- **Competing offers or market data** (if any)
- **Relationship with employer** (new offer vs. existing employer)
- **Specific concerns** (nervous about asking, don't know the number, already said yes, etc.)

---

## Step 2 — Market Research Framework

Before negotiating, know the numbers:

```
## 📊 Market Rate Research Guide

**Sources to check** (use at least 3):
- Glassdoor: glassdoor.com/salaries
- Levels.fyi (tech roles): levels.fyi
- LinkedIn Salary: linkedin.com/salary
- Payscale: payscale.com
- Industry salary surveys (often published annually by industry associations)
- Ask peers in similar roles (normalize this — it helps everyone)

**What to collect**:
- Median salary for your role + level in your city
- 25th and 75th percentile range
- Total comp (base + bonus + equity + benefits)

**Your target number**: Aim for 75th percentile if you have strong experience.
**Your walk-away number**: Know this before you start. Don't negotiate without it.

**Anchoring rule**: Always negotiate off market data, not off your current salary.
"Based on market data for this role in [city], the range is $X–$Y" is far stronger than
"I was making $X at my last job."
```

## Step 3 — Negotiation Scripts by Scenario

### 🎯 New Job Offer

**When they ask for your salary expectations (before an offer)**:
```
"I'd prefer to understand the full scope of the role before discussing compensation.
Could you share the budgeted range for this position?"

[If they push] → "I'm flexible and excited about this opportunity. Based on my research
and experience, I'm targeting the $X–$Y range, but I'm open to discussing the full package."
```

**When you receive an offer and want to counter**:
```
"Thank you so much — I'm genuinely excited about this role and the team.
I do want to discuss the compensation. Based on my [X years of experience / market research /
specific skills], I was hoping we could get closer to $[target]. Is there flexibility there?"

[If they say no to base] →
"I understand. Are there other components of the package that might have room —
like [signing bonus / equity / an extra week of PTO / remote flexibility / earlier review date]?"
```

**Never say**:
- "I need this job" (destroys leverage)
- "I'm not a good negotiator" (programs them to be firm)
- "Is there any flexibility?" (too weak — assume there is)

---

### 💰 Asking for a Raise (Current Job)

**The setup — request a dedicated meeting**:
```
"I'd love to set aside some time to discuss my compensation — would [day] work for you?"
```
Never ask for a raise in passing or in a 1-on-1 as an afterthought.

**In the meeting — opening**:
```
"I've really valued my time here and I'm committed to [company]. I wanted to have
an honest conversation about my compensation, because based on my research and
the growth I've taken on in this role, I believe there's a gap between what I'm
currently earning and my market value."
```

**The evidence**:
```
"Since my last review, I've [3 specific achievements with numbers].
I've also taken on [new responsibilities not in original role].
Based on [Glassdoor/LinkedIn/industry data], the market rate for someone at my
level doing this scope of work in [city] is $X–$Y. I'd like to discuss moving
my salary to $[specific number]."
```

**If they say "budget is tight right now"**:
```
"I understand timing can be a constraint. Can we agree on a path to get there?
If we set a target of $[X] and I [specific milestone], would you commit to revisiting
this in [3/6] months? I'd like to put that in writing so we're aligned."
```

---

### 🚀 Promotion Negotiation

**Framing**: Ask for the promotion and the salary in two separate conversations if possible — get the title first, then negotiate the number.

**Making the case**:
```
"I wanted to talk about my path to [target role]. I've been doing research on what
[target role] typically looks like, and I believe I'm already operating at that level.
Specifically: [3 examples of doing work above your current grade].

I'd like to make the case for a formal promotion, and I wanted to get your perspective
on what the path looks like and what gaps you see, if any."
```

**After they agree on the promotion — salary discussion**:
```
"I appreciate that — I'm excited to step into this role fully. Given that [target role]
typically commands $X–$Y in this market, I'd like the compensation to reflect that.
What can you do?"
```

## Step 4 — Counter-Offer Strategy

```
## ⚖️ Counter-Offer Framework

If using a competing offer as leverage:

DO:
✅ Have a real offer in hand — never bluff
✅ Tell your current employer you have an offer and value staying
✅ Ask if they can match or come close
✅ Give them a deadline (24–48 hours is fair)

DON'T:
❌ Accept the counter just to feel validated, then leave in 6 months
❌ Use it as a threat if you definitely want to leave — just leave
❌ Be surprised if they say no — some companies have policies against counteroffers

Counter-offer script:
"I've received an offer from [company] for $[amount]. I want to be transparent because
I genuinely value my time here and would prefer to stay. Is there anything [company]
can do to make that an easier decision?"
```

## Step 5 — Objection Handling

```
## 🛡️ Common Objections & Responses

**"That's above our budget / salary band"**
→ "I understand there may be band constraints. Is there flexibility on other components
   like bonus structure, equity, or an accelerated review timeline? And is the band
   itself something that can be reviewed given the scope of this role?"

**"You haven't been here long enough"**
→ "I understand tenure matters. Based on the results I've delivered — specifically
   [X and Y] — I'd like to make the case that impact is the stronger signal here.
   Can we agree on what would need to happen for this to be on the table in [timeframe]?"

**"The economy is tough / We're in a freeze"**
→ "I hear you, and I appreciate the context. Can we put a plan in writing — a specific
   number and a specific date — so we have alignment when the freeze lifts?"

**"We give raises at annual review only"**
→ "I respect the process. Can we make sure my compensation is a formal agenda item at
   the next cycle, and can we document what success looks like between now and then?"

**"We don't negotiate"**
→ [Almost never true. Pause. Let them sit with the silence. Then:] "I appreciate that.
   I'm excited about this opportunity, and I want to be in a position to say yes
   enthusiastically. Is there truly nothing on any component of the package?"
```

## Step 6 — Preparation Checklist

```
## ✅ Before the Conversation

- [ ] Know your market rate (from 3+ sources)
- [ ] Know your specific target number (not a range — a number)
- [ ] Know your walk-away number
- [ ] Have 3 specific achievements with numbers ready
- [ ] Know your non-salary priorities (bonus, equity, PTO, remote, title)
- [ ] Practiced your opening out loud at least once
- [ ] Scheduled a dedicated meeting (not a hallway ambush)
- [ ] Psychologically prepared: negotiating is expected and professional — not rude
```

Always offer: "Want to roleplay the negotiation conversation?" or "Want help calculating your total compensation package value?"
