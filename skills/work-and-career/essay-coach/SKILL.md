# Essay Coach Skill

Guide the user from blank page to polished essay with structured coaching at every stage.

## Step 1 — Identify the Essay Type & Stage

**Essay types** (each has different conventions):
- Academic essay (argumentative, analytical, expository, compare/contrast)
- College/university application essay
- Personal statement
- Reflective essay
- Research paper
- Professional/business writing

**Stage the user is at**:
- A) No idea where to start → go to Step 2
- B) Has a topic but no outline → go to Step 3
- C) Has an outline but no draft → go to Step 4
- D) Has a draft — needs feedback → go to Step 5
- E) Has a draft — needs final polish → go to Step 6

---

## Step 2 — Topic & Thesis Development

If starting from scratch, help them find their angle:

Ask:
- What is the essay question or prompt?
- What's your initial reaction or instinct about the topic?
- What do you actually think about this — what's your position?

Then guide toward a strong thesis:

```
## 🎯 Thesis Development

A strong thesis:
✅ Makes a specific, arguable claim (not a fact or observation)
✅ Tells the reader what you will argue AND why it matters
✅ Can be challenged — someone could reasonably disagree

Weak thesis: "Social media has both positive and negative effects."
→ This is an observation, not an argument.

Strong thesis: "While social media enables global connection, its algorithmic design
actively prioritizes engagement over wellbeing, making it a net negative for adolescent
mental health."
→ Specific, arguable, signals the essay's direction.

**Your draft thesis**: [user's attempt]
**Suggested improvement**: [refined version with explanation of what changed and why]
```

## Step 3 — Outline Building

Structure depends on essay type:

### Argumentative Essay (5-paragraph model, scalable)
```
## 📋 Essay Outline: [Title/Topic]

**Thesis**: [One clear sentence]

### Introduction
- Hook: [Attention-grabbing opening — statistic, anecdote, question, bold claim]
- Context: [2–3 sentences giving necessary background]
- Thesis statement: [Your argument]

### Body Paragraph 1 — [Strongest Argument]
- Topic sentence: [Mini-thesis for this paragraph]
- Evidence: [Fact, quote, statistic, example]
- Analysis: [Why does this evidence support your argument?]
- Transition: [Bridge to next paragraph]

### Body Paragraph 2 — [Second Argument]
- Topic sentence:
- Evidence:
- Analysis:
- Transition:

### Body Paragraph 3 — [Counterargument + Rebuttal]
- Acknowledge: [Strongest opposing view, stated fairly]
- Rebuttal: [Why your argument still holds]
- Evidence for rebuttal:

### Conclusion
- Restate thesis (in new words — not copy-paste)
- Synthesize key points (don't just summarize — show how they connect)
- Broader significance: Why does this matter beyond the essay?
- Closing image/call to action/thought-provoking statement
```

### College Application Essay
```
### Application Essay Structure
- **Opening scene**: Drop into a specific moment (not "Ever since I was young...")
- **The story**: What happened, what did you do?
- **The reflection**: What did you learn about yourself?
- **The connection**: How does this connect to who you are now / your future?
- **The forward look**: Where are you going? (optional — don't force it)

Key rule: Show, don't tell. "I am passionate about science" < "At 2am, I was still reading about black holes."
```

## Step 4 — Drafting Guidance

Give the user targeted advice to get words on paper:

```
## ✍️ Drafting Tips

1. **Write the body first** — introduction is easier once you know what you've argued
2. **One paragraph = one idea** — if you have two ideas in a paragraph, split it
3. **Every paragraph needs evidence** — claims without support are just opinions
4. **The "so what?" test** — after every claim, ask: why does this matter to my argument?
5. **Don't edit while drafting** — get the ideas out first, polish later

**Common first-draft problems to ignore for now**:
- Imperfect word choice (fix in revision)
- Awkward transitions (fix in revision)
- Introduction feels weak (rewrite last)
```

## Step 6 — Final Polish Checklist

```
## ✨ Final Polish Checklist

### Argument
- [ ] Thesis is in the introduction AND echoed in conclusion (different words)
- [ ] Every body paragraph has a topic sentence, evidence, and analysis
- [ ] Counterargument acknowledged and addressed (for argumentative essays)

### Style
- [ ] No sentence starts with "I" excessively (academic essays)
- [ ] Passive voice minimized ("The experiment was conducted" → "We conducted")
- [ ] No filler phrases: "It is important to note that", "In conclusion", "Throughout history"
- [ ] Varied sentence length — not all short, not all long
- [ ] No informal language: "a lot", "stuff", "things", "really", "very"

### Mechanics
- [ ] Citations formatted correctly (MLA / APA / Chicago — confirm which)
- [ ] Consistent tense throughout
- [ ] No comma splices or run-on sentences
- [ ] Read aloud — if you stumble, the sentence needs rewriting

### For Application Essays Specifically
- [ ] Does this sound like YOU, not a generic applicant?
- [ ] Did you show rather than tell?
- [ ] Is the opening line memorable?
- [ ] Have you answered the prompt (re-read it one last time)?
```

---

## Coaching Rules
- Always give specific feedback tied to the user's actual text — no generic comments
- Praise genuinely before critiquing — motivation matters
- Prioritize the 2–3 highest-impact changes, not a list of 20 issues
- For application essays: protect the student's voice — suggest, don't rewrite
- Offer to rewrite specific sentences or paragraphs as examples when helpful
