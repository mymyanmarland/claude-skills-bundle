# Email Drafter Skill

Write emails that get results — clear, appropriately toned, and strategically framed.

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

Ask if not provided:
- **Situation**: What is this email about? (follow-up, request, complaint, announcement, cold outreach, difficult conversation, etc.)
- **Recipient**: Who are they? (boss, peer, client, stranger, HR, vendor)
- **Goal**: What do you want the reader to DO after reading?
- **Tone**: Formal / Professional / Warm / Assertive / Apologetic
- **Key information to include**: Facts, dates, context the reader needs
- **Any prior context**: Is this a reply? Part of an ongoing conversation?

## Step 2 — Email Type Templates

### 📧 Follow-Up Email (no response received)

```
Subject: Following up — [Original topic]

Hi [Name],

I wanted to follow up on my email from [date/last week] regarding [topic].

[One sentence restating what you need or asked — don't make them search the thread]

Could you let me know [specific ask]? I'm happy to [make it easy for them — provide info, jump on a call, etc.].

Thanks for your time.

[Your name]
```

Rules: Send after 3–5 business days. One follow-up is fine; two is the limit. Never passive-aggressive.

---

### 📧 Cold Outreach Email

```
Subject: [Specific, personalized — not "Partnership Opportunity"]

Hi [Name],

[Personalized opener — reference their work, article, company, or mutual connection. 1 sentence.]

[Why you're reaching out — be direct and specific. 1–2 sentences.]

[Your value proposition — what's in it for them? 1–2 sentences.]

[Clear, low-friction ask — not "let's hop on a call for an hour" but "would a 15-minute chat next week work?"]

[Your name]
[Title] | [Company] | [Link if relevant]
```

Rules: Under 150 words. Subject line must NOT look like spam. One ask only.

---

### 📧 Difficult Conversation Email (raising a concern)

```
Subject: [Neutral, non-alarming — e.g., "Following up on our project timeline"]

Hi [Name],

I wanted to raise something that's been on my mind, and I hope we can work through it together.

[State the issue factually — no blame, no emotion. What happened, not why it's their fault.]

[Impact — how this is affecting work, the team, or you. Stick to professional impact.]

[What you'd like — a specific, reasonable ask for how to move forward.]

I'd welcome the chance to discuss this — happy to chat whenever works for you.

[Your name]
```

Rules: Never send when emotional — draft, wait 24 hours, review. Stick to facts. No CC-ing managers as a threat.

---

### 📧 Request to Boss / Senior Stakeholder

```
Subject: [Clear topic — e.g., "Request: Approval for X" or "Quick question re: Y"]

Hi [Name],

[Context in one sentence — why you're writing now]

[The ask — be direct. "I'd like to request..." or "I need your input on..."]

[Why it matters / business case — 1–2 sentences max]

[What you need from them specifically, and by when]

Happy to provide more detail if helpful.

Thanks,
[Your name]
```

Rules: Respect their time — make the ask in the first 3 lines. Give them a deadline to decide.

---

### 📧 Apology / Damage Control Email

```
Subject: Apology — [Brief description of what happened]

Hi [Name],

I want to apologize directly for [specific thing that went wrong].

[Acknowledge the impact — what it caused for them. Don't minimize.]

[Brief explanation — only if relevant and not an excuse. One sentence max.]

[What you're doing to fix it or prevent recurrence.]

I take full responsibility and am committed to making this right. Please let me know if there's anything further I can do.

Sincerely,
[Your name]
```

Rules: Lead with the apology — not with an explanation. Don't over-explain. Never "I'm sorry you felt...".

---

### 📧 Negotiation / Counter-offer Email

```
Subject: Re: [Job title] Offer — Following Up

Hi [Name],

Thank you so much for the offer — I'm genuinely excited about the opportunity to join [Company].

After careful consideration, I'd like to discuss the compensation package. Based on my [X years of experience / market research / competing offer], I was hoping we could explore a base salary closer to [$X].

I'm very motivated to make this work and confident I can contribute significantly from day one. Is there flexibility on the base, or are there other components of the package — such as [bonus / equity / remote days] — that might have room?

I look forward to finding a solution that works for both of us.

[Your name]
```

---

### 📧 Complaint / Escalation Email

```
Subject: Urgent: Issue with [Product/Service/Situation] — Account [#]

Dear [Name/Team],

I'm writing to formally raise a concern regarding [specific issue], which has been ongoing since [date].

[What happened — factual, chronological, specific. Dates, reference numbers, names.]

[Impact — how this has affected you: time lost, money, inconvenience, reputation.]

[What you expect as resolution — be specific: refund, replacement, apology, timeline.]

I'd like this resolved by [date]. If I don't hear back, I'll [next step — escalate to manager, file complaint, dispute charge].

[Your name]
[Contact info]
[Account/Order reference]
```

## Step 3 — Subject Line Formula

Subject lines determine open rates — write them last:

```
## ✉️ Subject Line Guide

**For internal emails**: [Action needed] + [Topic] + [Deadline if any]
→ "Approval needed: Q3 budget by Friday"

**For external/cold**: [Their name] + [specific hook] or [specific benefit]
→ "Quick question about your panel talk at [Event]"

**For follow-ups**: Simple and direct
→ "Following up — [Original topic]"

**For sensitive topics**: Neutral and non-alarming
→ "Checking in on our project" not "Complaint about team performance"

❌ Never: "Hi", "Quick question", "URGENT!!!", "You won't believe this"
```

## Step 4 — Polish Checklist

```
## ✅ Before You Send

- [ ] Is the ask crystal clear? Could the reader summarize what they need to do?
- [ ] Is the subject line specific enough to be found in search 6 months later?
- [ ] Is it under 200 words? (if it can be shorter, make it shorter)
- [ ] Did you proofread for tone — does it read how you want it to?
- [ ] Did you attach what you mentioned? ("Please see attached" with no attachment)
- [ ] Is the deadline explicit if there is one?
- [ ] Is the CC list correct — no one accidentally included or excluded?
- [ ] Are you sending this in the right emotional state?
```

Always offer: "Want a tougher or softer version of this?" or "Want 2–3 subject line options to choose from?"
