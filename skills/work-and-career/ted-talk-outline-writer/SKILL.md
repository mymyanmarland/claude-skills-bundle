# TED Talk Outline Writer Skill
## Step 1 — Gather Inputs
- **The central idea**: (what's the one idea they want to spread?)
- **Their expertise or personal experience with this topic**
- **Audience**: General / Industry / Academic / Community
- **Length**: 5 min / 10 min / 15 min / 18 min (standard TED max)
- **Tone**: Inspiring / Educational / Personal / Provocative / Scientific

## Step 2 — TED Talk Structure
```
## TED Talk: "[Title]"
Central idea: [One sentence — the idea that changes how someone thinks]
Length: [X minutes] | ~[X words at 130 words/min]

---

### THE OPENING (60-90 seconds)
[NOT "Hi, I'm X and today I'll talk about Y" — that's the #1 TED opener mistake]
[Start with: a story / a question / a surprising statement / a demonstration]
[The audience must feel immediately: "I need to hear this."]

Opening line: "[Draft of the first sentence]"

---

### THE SETUP (2-3 minutes)
[Establish the problem, gap, or tension that the talk resolves]
[Make the audience feel the stakes]
[Introduce yourself only insofar as it establishes WHY you of all people are saying this]

---

### THE JOURNEY (7-10 minutes)
[The meat of the talk — 2-3 major points or story beats]

**Act 1**: [The conventional wisdom or current state of affairs]
**The turn**: [The discovery, insight, or challenge that changes things]
**Act 2**: [The new framework, evidence, or story]
**The moment of connection**: [Where personal story and universal truth meet]

---

### THE PAYOFF (2-3 minutes)
[Deliver the central idea explicitly — now the audience is ready for it]
[Make it actionable: what can someone DO with this idea?]
[Widen the scope: why does this matter beyond the talk's topic?]

---

### THE CLOSE (60-90 seconds)
[Return to the opening image/story — the full circle]
[Leave them with one line they'll remember in the car]
[End on a note of possibility, not conclusion]

Closing line: "[Draft of the final sentence]"
```

## Step 3 — TED Talk Rules
- One idea. Not three, not five. One.
- The personal story earns the universal claim
- Slides (if used) show, they never tell — no bullet points
- The best talks make the audience feel something, then think something

Always offer: "Want me to write a full script for the opening 3 minutes?"
