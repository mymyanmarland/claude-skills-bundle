# Table Tennis Grip and Stance Skill

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

- Playing level: beginner/club/competitive
- Specific weakness or focus area
- Training environment available
- Age and physical development
- Match format played

## Step 2 — Core Output

TT grip and stance: Shakehand grip (most common — thumb and index finger on the rubber, other fingers wrap around handle) → Penhold grip (traditional and reverse penhold) → Grip pressure (relaxed until contact) → Stance (square or slightly sideways, feet shoulder-width, slight knee bend, weight forward) → Ready position (bat up and in front, slightly to forehand side) → Recovery to ready position.

## Step 3 — Quality & Polish

- Shakehand grip: most versatile for modern table tennis
- Relaxed grip: tension kills bat speed and feel
- Weight forward: enables faster reaction to close balls

Always offer a relevant next step or drill to practise.
