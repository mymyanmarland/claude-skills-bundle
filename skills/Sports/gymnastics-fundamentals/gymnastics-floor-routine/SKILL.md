# Gymnastics Floor Routine Development Skill

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

- Gymnastics experience level
- Apparatus or discipline focus
- Training environment: gym/club/home
- Age and physical development
- Competition or recreational goals

## Step 2 — Core Output

Floor routine development: Routine structure (intro / tumbling passes / dance elements / connecting skills / final pass) → Tumbling passes (skills connected in sequence — difficulty increases through the routine) → Dance elements (leaps, turns, split jumps — required elements in competition) → Connecting movements (between tumbling passes: not just walking) → Music (matches the athlete's personality and age) → Difficulty vs execution (a clean medium-difficulty routine scores higher than a messy hard one).

## Step 3 — Quality & Polish

- Clean medium difficulty beats messy hard: execution score matters more than D-score for developing gymnasts
- Music choice: age-appropriate always — this is strictly enforced in competition
- Dance elements: required by the code of points — cannot be ignored

Always offer a relevant next step or drill to practise.

📌 Sports Disclaimer: Gymnastics carries inherent risk of injury. Always train under a qualified gymnastics coach on appropriate equipment with proper matting. Never attempt new skills without qualified supervision and appropriate progressions.
