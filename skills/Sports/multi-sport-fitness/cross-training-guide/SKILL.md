# Cross-Training Guide Skill

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs

- Sports participated in
- Current fitness level
- Training hours available per week
- Primary performance goal
- Any injury history or concerns

## Step 2 — Core Output

Cross-training: Benefits (fitness maintenance / injury recovery / psychological freshness / exposing weaknesses) → Complementary activity selection (low impact for high impact sport athletes / different movement patterns / different energy system emphasis) → Cross-training options (swimming / cycling / rowing / yoga — all low injury risk, high fitness benefit) → Transfer to sport (some training transfers more than others — consider specificity) → When to use (injury recovery / overtraining prevention / off-season variety).

## Step 3 — Quality & Polish

- Injury recovery: cross-training maintains fitness while protecting the injury
- Psychological freshness: variety prevents burnout — especially during long seasons
- Transfer specificity: rowing transfers to rugby more than swimming transfers to tennis

Always offer a relevant next step or drill to practise.
