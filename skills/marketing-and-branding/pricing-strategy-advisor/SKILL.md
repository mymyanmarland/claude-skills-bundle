# Pricing Strategy Advisor Skill

Find the right price — and the confidence to charge it.

## Step 1 — Gather Inputs
- **What is being priced**: Product / Service / Course / Subscription / Consulting
- **Target customer and their budget range**
- **Competitor pricing** (if known)
- **Current price** (if already selling — and if it feels wrong, which direction)
- **Cost to deliver** (materials, time, overhead)
- **Value delivered**: What outcome does the customer get? What's that worth to them?

## Step 2 — Pricing Model Selection

```
## Pricing Model Options for [Product/Service]

### Option A: Cost-Plus Pricing
[Cost to deliver] × [Markup multiplier] = Price
Your cost: $[X] × [2-3×] = $[Y]
⚠️ Limitation: Ignores market value — often leads to underpricing

### Option B: Competitor-Based Pricing
Market range: $[Low] – $[High]
Positioning suggestion: [Below market (volume play) / At market (safe) / Above market (premium)]
Your price: $[X]
⚠️ Limitation: Anchors to competitors, not to your unique value

### Option C: Value-Based Pricing (recommended for services)
What outcome does the customer get? [Outcome]
What is that outcome worth to them? $[Value estimate]
Your price = 10-20% of that value: $[X] – $[Y]
✅ This is how premium brands and consultants price

### Option D: Tiered / Package Pricing
[See Step 3]
```

## Step 3 — Pricing Tiers Template

```
## Pricing Packages: [Product/Service]

### 🥉 [Tier Name — e.g., "Starter"]  $[Price]
Best for: [Customer type who needs this level]
Includes:
- [Deliverable 1]
- [Deliverable 2]
- [Deliverable 3]

### 🥈 [Tier Name — e.g., "Growth"] $[Price] ← MOST POPULAR
Best for: [Customer type]
Everything in Starter, plus:
- [Additional deliverable]
- [Additional deliverable]
- [Support level]

### 🥇 [Tier Name — e.g., "Premium"] $[Price]
Best for: [Customer type — often "serious/established"]
Everything in Growth, plus:
- [High-value addition]
- [VIP element]
- [Done-for-you or priority access]
```

**Anchoring principle**: The highest tier makes the middle tier feel reasonable. Most customers choose the middle.

## Step 4 — How to Raise Prices
```
## Raising Prices Without Losing Customers

1. **Give notice**: Announce 30-60 days ahead for existing clients
2. **Grandfather existing clients**: Honor their current rate for 6-12 months
3. **Frame the increase**: "Reflecting the expanded scope / market rate / value delivered"
4. **Raise for new clients first**: Test the higher price with new business
5. **Add value**: Bundle a new element that justifies the increase

Script for existing clients:
"As of [date], my pricing will be moving to $[X]. Existing clients will remain at $[current] 
through [date]. I deeply value our work together and wanted to give you plenty of notice."
```

## Step 5 — Pricing Psychology Tips
- Odd pricing: $97 feels cheaper than $100; $1,997 vs $2,000
- Annual discount: Offer 2 months free for annual payment (improves cashflow)
- Decoy pricing: Middle tier should be the obvious value winner
- Remove currency symbols where possible in premium contexts (cognitive research shows this increases spend)
