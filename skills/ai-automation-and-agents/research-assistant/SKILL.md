# Research Assistant Skill

Transform unfocused research into a structured, useful knowledge base.

## Step 1 — Clarify the Research Goal

Ask if not provided:
- **Topic**: What are you researching?
- **Purpose**: Academic paper / thesis / literature review / report / personal learning / professional research?
- **Scope**: Broad overview vs. deep dive on a specific angle?
- **Audience**: Who will read the final output?
- **Output needed**: Outline / annotated source list / full report / summary?
- **Existing material**: Do they have sources already, or starting from scratch?

---

## Step 2 — Research Question Sharpening

Help the user move from a broad topic to a focused, researchable question:

```
## 🔍 Research Question Development

**Your topic**: [Broad topic, e.g., "social media and mental health"]

**The problem**: This is too broad — thousands of papers cover this. We need to narrow it.

**Narrowing questions to ask yourself**:
- Which population? (teens, adults, elderly, specific gender?)
- Which platform? (Instagram, TikTok, all of them?)
- Which outcome? (depression, anxiety, loneliness, body image?)
- Which direction? (does social media cause it? correlate? what mediates it?)
- What time frame? (recent 5 years? longitudinal?)

**Draft research question**: [Refined version]
Example: "How does Instagram use correlate with body image dissatisfaction in adolescent girls aged 13–18?"

**Why this is better**:
- Specific population ✅
- Specific platform ✅
- Specific outcome ✅
- Researchable with existing literature ✅
```

## Step 3 — Source Strategy

Guide the user on WHERE and HOW to find good sources:

```
## 📚 Source Strategy for: [Topic]

### Best databases for this topic:
- **[Database 1]**: [e.g., PubMed — medical/health research]
- **[Database 2]**: [e.g., Google Scholar — broad academic search]
- **[Database 3]**: [e.g., JSTOR — humanities and social sciences]
- **[Database 4]**: [e.g., Scopus / Web of Science — peer-reviewed, citation tracking]

### Search terms to try:
- Primary: "[exact phrase]"
- Secondary: [synonym 1], [synonym 2]
- Combine with: AND, OR, NOT operators
- Filter by: peer-reviewed, last 5 years, English language

### Source quality hierarchy:
1. 🥇 Systematic reviews and meta-analyses (highest evidence)
2. 🥈 Randomized controlled trials / primary studies
3. 🥉 Observational studies / cohort studies
4. 📄 Review articles (good overview, not primary evidence)
5. 📰 Reputable news/journalism (context, not evidence)
6. ❌ Wikipedia, blogs, anonymous sources (background only, never cite)

### Red flags in sources:
- Published in predatory journals (check Beall's list)
- No peer review process
- Funded entirely by interested parties without disclosure
- Single study making extraordinary claims
```

## Step 4 — Literature Organization Framework

When the user has sources and needs to organize them:

```
## 🗂️ Literature Map: [Topic]

### Thematic Clusters

**Theme 1: [e.g., Prevalence and Epidemiology]**
- [Source 1]: [Key finding in one sentence]
- [Source 2]: [Key finding]
- [Source 3]: [Key finding]
*Consensus*: [What do these sources agree on?]
*Gaps*: [What's missing or disputed?]

**Theme 2: [e.g., Mechanisms / Causal Pathways]**
- [Source]: [Finding]
*Consensus*: [Agreement]
*Gaps*: [Disputes]

**Theme 3: [e.g., Interventions / Solutions]**
[Same structure]

---

### Chronological Development (for historical topics)
| Period | Key Developments | Landmark Studies/Events |
|--------|-----------------|------------------------|
| [Era 1] | [What changed] | [Key source] |
| [Era 2] | | |

---

### Contradictions & Debates in the Literature
| Issue | Position A | Position B | My Assessment |
|-------|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| [Debate 1] | [View] | [View] | [Which is stronger?] |
```

## Step 5 — Research Outline Builder

Turn organized research into a writing outline:

```
## 📋 Research Paper Outline: [Title]

**Research question**: [Your question]
**Thesis/argument**: [What you will argue based on the evidence]

---

### I. Introduction
- Hook: [Opening stat, quote, or scenario]
- Background: [Context needed to understand the issue]
- Research question: [State explicitly]
- Thesis: [Your argument]
- Roadmap: [Brief overview of paper structure]

### II. Literature Review / Background
- [Subtopic 1]: [Key sources + what they establish]
- [Subtopic 2]: [Key sources]
- [Gap in literature]: [What your paper addresses that others haven't]

### III. [Main Argument Section 1]
- Topic sentence
- Evidence: [Source + finding]
- Analysis: [Why this supports your thesis]
- Counterevidence: [Acknowledge conflicting findings]

### IV. [Main Argument Section 2]
[Same structure]

### V. [Main Argument Section 3]
[Same structure]

### VI. Discussion
- Synthesis of all arguments
- Implications of findings
- Limitations of current evidence
- Future research directions

### VII. Conclusion
- Restate thesis
- Summary of evidence
- Broader significance

### References
[Citation style: APA / MLA / Chicago / Vancouver]
```

## Step 6 — Citation Assistance

Help format references correctly:

```
## 📑 Citation Formatting

**APA 7th Edition**:
Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. *Journal Name*, *Volume*(Issue), pages. https://doi.org/xxxxx

**MLA 9th Edition**:
Author Last, First. "Article Title." *Journal Name*, vol. X, no. X, Year, pp. XX–XX.

**Vancouver** (medical):
1. Author AB, Author CD. Title of article. Abbreviated Journal Name. Year;Volume(Issue):pages.
```

## Step 7 — Research Integrity Reminders

```
## ⚠️ Research Ethics Checklist
- [ ] All sources cited — no uncited ideas (even if paraphrased)
- [ ] Primary sources checked — don't rely only on what other papers say about a study
- [ ] Conflicts of interest noted for key studies
- [ ] Correlation vs. causation not confused
- [ ] Limitations of your own argument acknowledged
- [ ] No cherry-picking — engage with contradictory evidence
```

Always offer: "Want me to help summarize any specific sources?" or "Want me to generate the full literature review section from this outline?"
